Dental Implants

A missing tooth can make you chew on one side, avoid certain foods, and hide your smile in photos. An implant is a way to fill that gap with a tooth that feels steady. A small post is placed in the jaw, and once it heals, a new tooth is fixed on top. Implants can replace one tooth, hold a bridge, or help a denture stay in place. The first step is a careful look at your bone, gums, and health.

Dr. Weiss will talk with you about whether an implant is a good fit, how many visits it may take, and what healing looks like. You will get clear steps for food, rest, and cleaning after each visit. Once the new tooth is in place, brush and floss around it like a real tooth, and keep your checkups. The team will watch the gum and bone near the implant and catch small changes early. With steady care, an implant can help you enjoy meals and talk with ease.

General Dentistry

General care is the base of a healthy mouth. Checkups, cleanings, fillings, and gum care keep small problems from growing. During a checkup, Dr. Weiss looks at each tooth, checks your gums, and talks about what he finds. A cleaning clears plaque and tartar that a brush can miss. A small cavity can be filled before it turns into an ache. If your gums bleed when you brush, the team can show you a gentler way to clean and watch how they heal.

Most people do well with a checkup and cleaning twice a year, but your plan may differ. Some people build tartar fast. Some have gum trouble that needs closer watch. Some take medicines that dry the mouth. At home, brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste, clean between your teeth once a day, and drink water after sweet snacks. Tell the team about any change in your health, and bring your medicine list to each visit.

Cosmetic Dentistry

Cosmetic care is about how your smile looks when you talk, laugh, and meet people. It can cover whitening, bonding, and veneers. Whitening can lift stains from coffee, tea, and time. Bonding can fix a small chip in one visit with a tooth-colored material. Veneers are thin shells that sit on the front of teeth to change shape or color. The right choice depends on your teeth, your bite, and what you want to see.

Start with a talk about what bothers you. Is it color, a chip, a gap, or a tooth that looks out of line? Dr. Weiss will show you what each choice can and cannot do, how long it may last, and how to care for it. A whitening plan may need touch-ups over time. Bonding may need care when you bite hard foods. Veneers need gentle brushing and regular checks. The aim is a smile that looks like you, only brighter and more even.

Oral Surgery

Some dental needs call for surgery, such as a tooth that must come out, a wisdom tooth that causes pain or crowding, or work that gets the mouth ready for an implant. Before the day comes, the team will walk you through what will happen, how long it may take, and how to heal at home. You will know what to eat, how to rest, and when to call if something feels wrong.

After surgery, rest is part of the care. Choose soft foods, sip cool drinks, and take medicine as told. Follow the care sheet, and keep the area clean in the way the team shows you. A little swelling or soreness is common at first. If bleeding stays heavy, if pain grows after a few days, or if you notice a bad taste that will not go away, call the office. The team can check the area and help you heal well.

Emergency

Tooth pain does not wait for a good time. It can show up at work, on a trip, or in the middle of the night. If a tooth breaks, a filling falls out, or pain keeps you up, call (561) 260-5575 as soon as you can. Tell the team what happened, when it started, and how bad it feels. They will help you know what to do next and find a time to be seen. If a tooth is knocked out, hold it by the crown, do not scrub it, keep it moist, and call right away.

While you wait, a cold pack on the outside of the cheek can help with swelling, and warm salt water can soothe a sore spot. Do not put a pain pill right on the gum, and do not try to fix a broken tooth on your own. If you have swelling in the face, trouble opening your mouth, or a fever with tooth pain, get help fast. The goal of an urgent visit is to ease the pain, find the cause, and make a plan that keeps the tooth safe when it can be saved.

Invisalign

Clear trays move teeth a little at a time, without wires or brackets. Many adults and teens like them because they are hard to see, they come out for meals and brushing, and there is nothing to poke the cheek. The first step is a look at your bite, your teeth, and your goals. If clear trays are a good fit, Dr. Weiss will map out the steps and show you what the trays will do over time. You will wear each set for about one to two weeks, then move to the next set.

Clear trays work best when you wear them as told, usually for most of the day and night. Take them out to eat and drink anything but water, brush before you put them back in, and keep them in their case when they are out of your mouth. If a tray feels tight for a day or two, that is often a sign it is doing its job. If it hurts a lot, cracks, or does not fit, call the office and ask what to do next. Straight teeth can be easier to clean, and a better bite can feel more calm when you chew.

How a Checkup Can Catch Trouble Early

A checkup is more than a cleaning. It is a chance to catch small changes before they turn into pain. Dr. Weiss looks for cavities, gum trouble, worn teeth, and spots that need watch. He also looks at how your teeth meet when you bite, and whether a tooth is cracking under stress. Catching these things early often means simpler care, less pain, and a lower cost. That is why a Dental office Palm Beach Gardens visit twice a year is worth the time, even when nothing hurts.

Between visits, watch for signs that need a call. Bleeding gums, a tooth that feels loose, pain when you drink something cold, or a sore that will not heal are all worth a check. Do not wait for the pain to get worse. A short call can tell you whether you need to come in soon or whether it can wait until your next visit. Your mouth gives you clues, and the team can help you read them.
